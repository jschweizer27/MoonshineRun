Baltimore, 1922 · The Volstead Act is law. Liquor is gold.

You are Otto Braun. Last night the Temperance Alliance burned your Highlandtown brewery to the ground. What's left is a truck, your father's old barn up the Green Spring Valley, and the roads out of Baltimore, littered with what other people lost: crates, kegs, copper. Work the roads, pack what you find into the truck, sell it where it pays best, and buy back what was yours.

Drive over glowing loot to pick it up, fit it into the trunk, then stop at a ⬢ market to sell and upgrade the truck.

HOW TO PLAY

  1. The story comes in chapters. The banner shows the next step (★ on the radar for a place in the story) when there's no chase, order or cargo to see to. In Chapter 1, Ashes, search the ruins of Braun & Sons in Highlandtown for Father's copper coil, get the still running, win Gus Kessler's trust, and go back to the cellar after dark. Each chapter ends with a clue to who paid for the fire, kept in your notebook (B).
  2. Find salvage: wrecks, abandoned farmhouses, rail sidings and city cellars. A SALVAGE sign marks the nearest, ⊗ marks them on the radar. Stop beside one to work it: pry as the needle crosses the green, or search where you saw the goods glint. The better you do, the more you get. A worked site is empty for a day or two, and the best open only at night.
  3. What you find goes into the trunk. Move it, turn it (R, or LB/RB) and fit it in; pieces can't overlap. Leave behind what won't fit.
  4. With loot aboard, the arrow and the gold route on the radar lead to the nearest ⬢ market. Stop there to sell: Lexington Market in Baltimore, the Monkton General Store up York Road, or the Glyndon Depot out west. Out east, the Cockeysville Quarry Store pays best for copper, kegs and barrels, once you're a Runner. Each pays differently, the price drops as you sell more of one thing, and each day one town posts a demand: double for one kind of loot.
  5. Spend the cash on upgrades at any market: a bigger bed, a tuned engine, stiffer springs, a longer reach, a spotter for the radar and, later, a false bottom.
  6. The roads are busy: traffic to dodge (a crash wears the truck and can spill a piece), and now and then a washed-out or blocked road (⚠ on the radar), a fog bank, or a town's market day, when it pays more for everything.
  7. Otto's barn (⌂ on the radar, just off York Road outside the city) is home: store loot in its stash, and mend the truck in its garage. Hard knocks wear the truck and slow it down.
  8. At the barn's still, turn loot into shine: install a copper coil, then brew from what you've gathered (Corn Shine takes a sack and jugs). A batch is three steps: keep the fire in its band, cut into the hearts and out again (cut too early and it's poison), then read the proof. It's graded A to C, and each recipe's crates on hand are one blend that sells by its grade. Pour out a bad batch, or it taints the blend and blinds someone. Jars, bottles and shine break in a hard crash. Shine sells only at the city's speakeasies: with crates aboard, the radar shows them.
  9. Revenue agents patrol the roads (more at night), each watching the road ahead in a cone. If one sees shine aboard, you have heat (★ on the HUD): they follow, then radio for a second car and a roadblock, then every car in the county comes. Get out of their sight to lose them. Boxed in and stopped, you're busted: the shine is taken, you're fined, and you wake at the barn. At night, turn the headlamps off (L, or d-pad down) to be harder to spot. A checkpoint stands at the York Road gap at night: stop and they search the trunk, or run it and they chase. A false bottom (an upgrade, from Runner) hides a couple of crates.
  10. Orders: the people at the speakeasies and the farms post orders each day (on the board at any market, speakeasy or the barn), and from Brewer on, Sheriff Hale sends for things to the county lockup in Cockeysville. Keep up to three in your book. Farms want goods, any time before the order is due. Once you brew, the speakeasies want shine of a grade, handed over only after dark and by dawn. Stop at their door to deliver. The further from the barn, the more an order pays. Each contact's trust (★ to ★★★★★) grows with every order kept, and better shine earns more. Trusted contacts pay more and ask for more, and in time they tell you things. Sheriff Hale, once he trusts you, can make a bust in the county go away for a price. Your notebook (B, or the pause menu) keeps your orders, contacts, recipes and clues.
  11. Your name earns ranks, from Junk Hauler to King of York Road. Each unlocks something: recipes, better upgrades, and abilities (keys 1 to 3, the d-pad, or the chips by the speedometer): the Jockey's Tip, Lead Foot and Sweet Talk. At the top, the bank will sell you back the Braun & Sons deed at Lexington Market.
